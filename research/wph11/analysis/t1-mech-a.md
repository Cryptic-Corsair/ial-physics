# WPH11 Topic 1 mechanics gap analysis — agent a (2020-Jan → 2021-Jun)

Question-by-question coverage check against the site's WPH11 notes (T1 specs 1–22 in notes-ph01-a/b/c.ts; T2 specs 23–32 in notes-ph02-a/b.ts, consulted where a mechanics question leans on a materials fact).

Question asked by the site owner: *"Could a student who has read ONLY our revision notes gain ALL the marks on every question in these papers?"*

Verdict key: **COVERED** = taught method/fact applied in a new context (name the section); **PARTIAL** = ingredients taught but a specific element missing; **GAP** = the notes never teach it. Blocks labelled "Mark scheme wording", "Examiner's report", "Not accepted" in the notes are treated as taught knowledge. MS quotes are verbatim from the mark-scheme txt files.

## Session 1 — 2020 January (WPH11/01, 13 Jan 2020)

### Section A MCQs (Q1–10, 1 mark each)

**Q1 — MCQ — SI base units (power).** Stem: "Which of the following quantities has the SI base units kg m2 s−3?" (A force, B momentum, C power, D work done).
Knowledge needed: express power/force/momentum/work done in SI **base** units (kg m² s⁻³, kg m s⁻², kg m s⁻¹, kg m² s⁻²) and derive them from definitions (W = J/s = N m/s; N = kg m s⁻²).
MS distractor-facts: "A is not the correct answer as units would be kg m s-2 / B … kg m s-1 / D … kg m2 s-2".
**VERDICT: GAP.** The notes give derived units (1 J = 1 N m, 1 W = 1 J s⁻¹, momentum in kg m s⁻¹ / N s, and the note that kg m s⁻² "is a newton") but never teach SI **base** units or the base-unit decomposition of any quantity. Nothing in specs 1–22 derives kg m² s⁻³.

**Q2 — MCQ — gravitational field strength definition.** "Which statement describes the gravitational field strength acting on a body at a point?" Answer B "gravitational force per unit mass". Distractors: force per unit length ("no meaning"), "this is the gravitational force" (C), "this is gravitational potential" (D).
**VERDICT: COVERED** — spec 10 def card: "The weight per unit mass, g = F ÷ m, measured in N kg⁻¹", plus spec 10 "Not accepted" note (acceleration/energy per unit mass rejected). The g = F/m definition is exactly the printed answer.

**Q3 — MCQ — direction of the change in velocity (vector diagram).** Stem: velocity before/after diagrams, "The magnitude of the velocity did not change. Which of the following arrows represents the direction of the change in velocity?" Answer B.
MS reasons: "A is not the correct answer as the magnitude of the final velocity would be greater and a little less to the right. / C … the final velocity would be greater and to the left of the original velocity. / D … similar to C, but more to the left and of a lesser magnitude."
Knowledge: Δv is a **vector** = (final velocity) − (initial velocity); in 2D built by adding the reversed initial velocity tip-to-tail.
**VERDICT: GAP.** The notes teach tip-to-tail vector **addition** (spec 6) and signed subtraction of *momenta* (spec 13/14: "a change-of-momentum question is a subtraction with signs"), but never state that a **change in velocity** is itself a vector obtained by subtracting the initial velocity, nor how to construct Δv geometrically. Grep confirms "change in velocity" appears only as the area-under-a-t meaning (spec 3 table).

**Q4 — MCQ — ΣF = ma on a ball travelling upwards with air resistance.** "A ball of mass m is projected vertically upwards. An air resistance F acts on the ball at all times. Which equation would apply … as it is travelling upwards?" Answer D mg + F = ma. Distractor reasons: velocity not constant (A, B); "the air resistance does not act in the opposite direction to gravity when an object travels upwards" (C).
**VERDICT: COVERED** — spec 9 (ΣF = ma; identify the resultant; drag opposes motion) + T2/A spec 25 "Rising objects": "The drag on an object always acts in the direction opposite to its motion: downwards on a falling object, upwards on a rising object." Weight and drag both act downwards on the way up.

**Q5 — MCQ — positions of P and Q at t = 4 s from v–t graphs.** Answer D ("Q is further from the initial position than P"). Distractor reasons: "the areas under the two graphs are not both zero" (A); "areas … not equal" (B); "P is at the initial position" (C).
Knowledge: displacement = area under v–t; areas below the axis count negative; compare |displacement|.
**VERDICT: COVERED** — spec 3 "Slopes and areas" table: velocity–time … "displacement (areas below the axis count negative)".

**Q6 — MCQ — contact time at a wall (sign convention in v = u + at).** Ball at 7.0 m s⁻¹ rebounds at 5.0 m s⁻¹, a = 300 m s⁻²; which expression for t. Answer D t = (5−7)/−300.
**VERDICT: COVERED** — spec 1 suvat + spec 14 sign convention ("one direction positive, the other negative"; the gliders card shows the reversal arithmetic and records "a common error was to use positive values of momentum for both gliders" — the same sign discipline).

**Q7 — MCQ — trolleys m and 3m collide and stick.** Answer D ("The speed of X after the collision is less than v"). Distractor reasons: A/B "contradict Newton's Third Law"; C "the force of Y on X is in the opposite direction to the velocity of X".
**VERDICT: COVERED** — spec 14 worked card "Two gliders stick together" is structurally identical (opposite velocities, stick together, direction verdict from the sign of total momentum); spec 12 for the N3 distractors.

**Q8 — MCQ — efficiency (energy supplied to a motor).** 68 % efficient motor raises 350 N through 25 m; which expression gives energy supplied. Answer D (350×25)/0.68. Distractor reasons: A/B give J⁻¹; "C … 68 ≠ 68%".
**VERDICT: COVERED** — spec 22 "Input from output: input = output ÷ efficiency … multiplying by the efficiency instead is the rejected option" (escalator card uses the decimal 0.63 the same way).

**Q9 — MCQ — force–extension graph points P/Q/R (limit of proportionality, elastic limit, yield point).**
**VERDICT: Materials — skipped** (spec 29; another agent's territory).

**Q10 — MCQ — why the gradient of a v–t graph of a falling object decreases.** Answer A "The drag force increases". Distractor reasons: gravitational force does not increase; warmer liquid → lower viscosity → wouldn't explain; "the upthrust depends on the density of the fluid and the volume of the sphere, neither of which changes".
**VERDICT: COVERED (mechanics part of a mixed question)** — spec 2 (gradient of v–t = acceleration) + spec 9 terminal-velocity chain step 3 ("As the velocity increases, the drag force … increases"); the upthrust-constancy fact is in T2/A spec 25.

**Q3 — MCQ — roller coaster P (75 m) → Q (30 m); which expression gives the velocity at Q.** Answer D (√(2×45g) = √(90g)). MS: "A is not the correct answer as would be difference in velocities of cars descending to ground from standing starts at P and Q and is out by factor of √2 / B is not the correct answer as would be difference in velocities of cars descending to ground from standing starts at P and Q / C is not the correct answer as is out by a factor of √2".

**Q4 — MCQ — change of velocity with signs.** "A car travels north with a velocity of +50 m s−1. While still travelling north, the car slows to a velocity of +20 m s−1. **Q5 — MCQ — which scaled vector diagram is the correct resultant of P and Q.** Answer D. Distractor reasons: "R has the wrong direction" (A, B); "R is along the wrong diagonal" (C).
**VERDICT: COVERED** — spec 6 steps panel: "Close the triangle — The resultant runs from the tail of the first vector to the tip of the last, and its arrow points that way round." The MS distractor reasons are exactly the errors the notes describe.

**Q6 — MCQ — energy supplied to a lamp (efficiency 0.68, useful 120 J).** Answer D E = 120/0.68. Distractor reasons: "spurious factor of 100", "gives a lower input than output".
**VERDICT: COVERED** — spec 22 input-from-output rule and the multiplication trap named there.

**Q7 — MCQ — CORE PRACTICAL 1 (fewest measurements to get g).** "A student used a falling sphere … released from rest. **Q8 — MCQ — work done stretching a wire from σ, A, Δx.**

**Q9 — MCQ — describe the motion from a v–t graph of a dropped ball that rebounds.** Answer B ("dropped and rebounds to a lower position").
