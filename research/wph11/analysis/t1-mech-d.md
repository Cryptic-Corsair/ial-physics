**Q4 (MCQ, 1) — MECHANICS.** Cyclist at constant speed on the flat, then the same speed up a constant-gradient hill; choose the P–t graph (D: constant non-zero power, then an increase to a new constant).
- Knowledge: at constant speed on the flat the driving force only balances drag, so P = Fv is a constant > 0; up the hill at the same speed an extra mg·sinθ component joins, so P rises to a higher constant. MS distractor reasons: "the initial power output should be a constant greater than zero, and the power output whilst going uphill should be constant"; "whilst going uphill the power output should be constant"; "the power output should increase".
- VERDICT: **COVERED** — SPEC 21 "A boat at constant speed" card teaches P = Fv with F = drag at constant speed on the flat (2.6 × 10⁴ W worked example); the SPEC 21 "A BMX track" card teaches "Stage B: constant velocity up the ramp — … P = mgv: ==constant==". Both marking ingredients are taught; the question applies them in a new context.

**Q5 (MCQ, 1) — MECHANICS.** Table-tennis ball dropped; t and v calculated with the equations of motion (which ignore air resistance); how do the actual t and v compare? (C: actual t greater, actual v less.)

- VERDICT: **COVERED** — SPEC 11 error table: "Air resistance and upthrust present · g too small (a < g)"; feeding a < g into the taught suvat equations gives both consequences. The terminal-velocity chain (SPEC 9) reinforces "acceleration decreases … velocity is still increasing".

**Q6 (MCQ, 1) — MECHANICS.** Bird of weight W on the middle of a string between vertical posts, string at 80° to each post; which expression gives T? (C: W ÷ (2 cos 80°).)
- VERDICT: **COVERED** — verbatim worked card in SPEC 5 ("A bird on a wire"), which also quotes the printed rejected-option note: "the components of T acting vertically should be cos and should form the denominator" — all four MS distractor reasons are pre-taught.

**Q10 (MCQ, 1) — MECHANICS.** Object from rest, uniform acceleration for time t; a–t graph shown, X = area under it from 0 to t; which expression gives the distance? (D: Xt/2.)
- Knowledge: area under a–t = change in velocity, so X = at (u = 0); then s = ½at² = ½Xt (or s = ½(u + v)t with v = X). MS distractor reasons: A "gives the change in velocity of the object"; B "gives the average velocity"; C "gives twice the distance travelled".

**Q1 (MCQ, 1) — MECHANICS.** Mine engine transfers energy at 180 kW and wastes 60 kW; which expression gives the efficiency? (A: 180/240.)

- VERDICT: **COVERED** — SPEC 2 table rows "v–t: straight line · constant acceleration", "v–t: curve levelling to horizontal · acceleration falling to zero", "a–t: any value above zero · velocity still increasing — even while acceleration itself falls"; area = displacement is in SPEC 3. - VERDICT: **COVERED** — SPEC 4 lists mass, work done, energy as scalars and momentum as a vector; the "unit test" bullet and the SPEC 13 def (momentum = mass × velocity, a vector) settle it.

**Q10 (MCQ, 1) — MECHANICS.** Ship of mass 1.2 × 10⁷ kg accelerating at 0.03 m s⁻²; engine force 8.0 × 10⁵ N; which expression gives the drag force? (A: 8.0 × 10⁵ − (0.03 × 1.2 × 10⁷).)

- MS verbatim: "3 The only correct answer is D (The displacement at 4 hours divided by a time of 4 hours) 1 · A is not correct because the area under a displacement-time graph has no significance · B is not correct because this would give the instantaneous speed at the end of the marathon · C is not correct because this would give the instantaneous speed at 2 hours".

**Q5 (MCQ, 1) — MECHANICS.** Box slides down a slope at constant speed; why? (A: "The component of the weight parallel to the slope is equal to the sum of the resistive forces".)

## GAPS SUMMARY

1. **PARTIAL — Average quantities from motion graphs (chord vs tangent) are half-taught.** The notes teach "tangent = instantaneous velocity/acceleration" and "area under an s–t graph has no physical quantity", but never state that the gradient of the chord — equivalently displacement ÷ total time — is the AVERAGE velocity (nor the matching average-acceleration phrasing). Occurrence: **2024-Oct Q3** (MCQ, runner's displacement–time graph, "Which of the following gives the average velocity of the person?"), where the MS answer is "D (The displacement at 4 hours divided by a time of 4 hours)" and the distractor reasons are "B is not correct because this would give the instantaneous speed at the end of the marathon" and "C is not correct because this would give the instantaneous speed at 2 hours". (2024-Jan Q16(a), "Determine the average acceleration of the skittle during the collision", is scoreable with the taught Δv ÷ Δt method, but "average" is likewise never glossed.) One added sentence in SPEC 2/3 closes this.
2. **PARTIAL — Resultant of two equal forces at a printed angle between them.** The notes teach the add-two-symmetric-components pattern only as equilibrium equations with the angle measured to a drawn reference line (SPEC 5 "Traffic lights" 2T sin θ = W; "Bird on a wire" 2T cos 80° = W). They never state that the resultant of two equal forces lies along the bisector of the angle between them, so the printed angle must be halved before using cos. Occurrence: **2024-Jun Q19(b)(i)** — "the tension in the cable is 1200 N and the angle formed by the cable at the wheel is 144°… Show, by calculation, that the force on the wheel from the cable is about 740 N. (2)", MS: "Appropriate trigonometry used (1); 742 (N) (1); Example of calculation R = 2 × 1 200 N × cos (72°) = 741.6 N". One sentence + one worked line in SPEC 5/6 closes this.
3. **PARTIAL — Stating the "resistive forces negligible" assumption in energy explanations.** The notes teach the mass-independence chain (ΔEgrav = ΔEk, mass cancels, v = √(2gΔh)) but never teach that, when a stem does not print "resistive forces ignored", the model answer should state the assumption that work done against resistive forces is negligible. Occurrence: **2024-Jun Q19(c)** — "Explain why the speeds of people with different masses are always about the same when they reach the low point. (3)", MS third marking point: "(assuming) work done against resistive forces is negligible (1)" (first two points: "(most) Egrav transferred to Ek and mass is in both equations (1)"; "(So final) speed does not depend on mass of person … Or … speed = √2g∆h (and g is constant) (1)"). One bullet in SPEC 20 closes this.

No outright GAPs were found in these three sessions: every other mechanics mark traces to a def card, equation band, table row, steps panel, worked card, or quoted mark-scheme/examiner block in notes-ph01-a/b/c.ts.
