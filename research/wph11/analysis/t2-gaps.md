# WPH11 Topic 2 (Materials) gap synthesis against the current notes

Question: *"Could a student who has read ONLY our revision notes gain ALL the marks on every Materials question in the 2020-2024 papers?"*

Method: every Materials question documented in agent-1..agent-5 was listed, the knowledge/skill each mark requires was extracted from the printed MS points, and each was checked against the **current** notes via the coverage baseline plus targeted greps of the real note files (`notes-ph02-a.ts`, `notes-ph02-b.ts`, `notes-ph01-a/b/c.ts`). Blocks labelled "Mark scheme wording", "Examiner's report" and "Not accepted" were treated as taught knowledge.

**Questions judged: 161 Materials question entries** (some are parts of mixed Topic-1/Topic-2 questions). Of these, **92 sit in the owner's 2020-2024 mandate window** (2020-Jan → 2024-Oct, 14 sessions); 18 are 2019 and 51 are 2025-2026 (judged too, flagged "outside window").

Bottom line: the current notes are very strong — nearly every MS point in every session traces to a taught bullet, often verbatim (the CP2 "one other measurement" reject-list, the v–d² straight line, the per-unit-volume derivation, the hydrometer "any two from", the submarine/cartesian-diver/Galilean narratives, the unit family, the deduce-with-consistent-convention pattern). **Four items in the 2020-2024 window are not fully covered (1 of them is an outright error in the notes), plus 4 outside-window items.** Details per session below, then the conventions sweep, then the deduplicated GAPS SUMMARY.

Legend: ✔ COVERED (location in the notes) · ◐ PARTIAL · ✘ GAP · ⚠ notes error.

## Mark-scheme conventions the current notes never state

Checked against BOTH topics' "The marking rules that never change" (T1 table: Use of / ecf / sig figs / units / g / deduce / reverse working / bald answers; T2 bullets: Use of, (1) per point, ecf, dependent marks, Or-joins, deduce-consistent-conclusion, show-that sf + reverse cap, g = 9.81/10, graph tolerances + reading-first dependency, starred 4+2 grid, MCQ distractor reasons). The following conventions appear in the analyses but in neither rules section:

1. **Case of unit symbols not penalised** (printed in every MS general-guidance block).
2. **"[no ue]" flags** — specific answers exempt from the unit penalty (e.g. 2021-Oct 14(b)(i) "Gradient = 18 500 (N m⁻¹) (sf range 18 - 19, no ue)").
3. **General "Max N" caps** where more creditable points than marks are listed ("Max 2" 2022-Jan 17(a); "Max 3" 2023-Oct 18(b)(i); "Max 6" 2019-Jun 17(b)). The notes teach the cap only for the Stokes-conditions list.
4. **Fallback marks** — "If no other mark scored, allow 1 mark for…" (2023-Oct 18(b)(i); 2024-Jan Q13 density fallback; 2019-Oct Q16(a) energy fallback).
5. **"Ignore …" guidance** — tolerated-noise lists ("[Ignore references to viscous drag.]" 2023-Oct 17(c); "(ignore references to greater risk of flooding)" 2019-Oct Q14; "Ignore the bubble may not be small" 2025-Oct Q17).
6. **"Allow converse argument"** (2024-Jun 14(c)) — the notes teach both directions of the temperature argument, so the physics is safe, but the convention itself is not stated.
7. **The graduated starred-question IC ladder** — the notes state only "6 IC points → 4 marks + 2 structure"; the printed tables also give 5 IC → 3 (+linkage 1 → 4), 4 → 3 (+1 → 4), 3 → 2 (+1 → 3), 2 → 2, 1 → 1 (e.g. 2022-Oct Q14 table).
8. **Reverse-check lines "(show that value gives …)"** printed next to ecf answers — a reassuring detail of the show-that rule that is not quoted.

None of these change what a well-taught student would *write*; they cost marks only at the margin (e.g. not knowing a fallback exists, or panicking over a "Max 2" list). The knowledge gaps below are the ones that cost real marks.

## GAPS SUMMARY

1. **⚠ ERROR in the notes — drag direction on a rising object.** The current notes' Spec 25 "Rising objects" opening bullet reads: *"The drag on an object always acts in the direction opposite to its motion: downwards on a falling object, upwards on a rising object"* (`notes-ph02-a.ts`, Spec 25 block) — inverted physics (it contradicts the correct bullet two lines down, "A rising bubble has the upthrust acting upwards and the drag acting downwards on it"). A student following the first sentence draws the drag arrow the wrong way. Occurrences: **2021-Jan Q18(a)** (2-mark FBD of a bead rising through oil; MS: *"Weight Or W, downwards / Drag Or D, downwards"*), and the same physics is needed in 2023-Jun Q16 (air bubble), 2025-Oct Q5 MCQ (U = W + D), 2025-Oct-01A/2026-Jun rising-bubble questions. Fix: rewrite the bullet as "upwards on a falling object, downwards on a rising object". **2020-2024.**

2. **Exceeding the elastic limit can permanently change (reduce) an object's stiffness — never taught.** The notes teach only the ideal unloading model ("the unloading line runs ==parallel to the loading line==", Spec 29) and never that overstressing can leave a spring with a *different, smaller* stiffness, so a student has no taught answer for why an unloading gradient differs. Occurrence: **2020-Oct Q16(a)(iii)** — MS: *"The spring constant/stiffness (not Young modulus) is smaller or has changed (do not allow greater stiffness)"*; same fact underlies 2019-Jun Q17(b) *"The old rope is not as stiff as the new rope"* (outside window). Fix: add one bullet after the parallel-unloading bullet. **2020-2024.**

3. **Vernier callipers are explicitly REJECTED for measuring wire diameter — never stated.** The notes say diameter is measured "with a ==micrometer screw gauge== (or digital callipers)" (Spec 28/31) and elsewhere endorse the vernier scale (0.1 mm) for extension readings, so a student can plausibly offer a vernier calliper for the diameter and lose the mark. Occurrence: **2021-Jan Q16(a)** — MS: *"Micrometer (screw gauge) Or digital (not Vernier) calliper(s)"*. Fix: add "(a vernier calliper is not accepted — its 0.1 mm resolution is too coarse for a wire diameter)". **2020-2024.**

4. **Anomalous readings must be discarded before averaging — never taught.** The notes say "average several readings" but never teach excluding an anomalous value (or that the mean diameter then goes into A = π(d/2)²). Occurrence: **2019-Oct Q3 MCQ** — MS: *"D is not correct as the anomalous point (1.36) has not been ignored."* Fix: one bullet in Spec 28 "Measuring the wire". **Outside window** (2019-Oct), but one line fixes any future mean-of-readings item.

5. **Boat stability = the restoring moment of the upthrust (centre of buoyancy) — never taught as a moment argument.** The notes' ballast bullet stops at "The centre of gravity of the displaced water moves lower, which makes the boat more stable"; they never teach that the upthrust acts through the centre of the displaced volume and its moment Ud about G rights the boat, nor the G-higher → d-smaller → less-stable chain. Occurrences: **2019-Oct Q14(a)(i)-(ii)** — MS: *"(This moment) causes an anti-clockwise rotation/motion (about G)… Returning/maintaining the boat to/in an upright/initial position"*; *"The distance d is reduced… The boat would be less stable / The boat could turn over"*. Fix: extend the Ballast-tanks block with the Ud restoring-moment chain (it reuses taught T1 moments + taught upthrust line-of-action). **Outside window** (2019-Oct).

6. **Qualitative "larger area in the water → larger drag" — never taught outside Stokes' law.** The notes teach drag only through Stokes' law (spheres, 6πηrv); the thin-boat-vs-full-ballast drag disadvantage needs the plain statement that a greater submerged/cross-sectional area gives a greater resistance. Occurrence: **2019-Oct Q14(b)(ii)** — MS: *"Greater (surface) area of boat in contact with water Or greater cross-sectional area in water (in direction of travel) / There a greater resistance/drag/friction (on the boat)."* Fix: one bullet in the Ballast-tanks block. **Outside window** (2019-Oct).

7. **"Greater Young modulus" can be rejected as an explanation even when E has genuinely changed — the notes' warning covers only the wrong-gradient context.** The notes' Not-accepted bullet (Spec 29) rejects "a greater Young modulus" as the reason for a changed gradient of one sample; the 2019 mark scheme additionally rejects it for the dried-concrete discussion, where E really did rise but the creditable language is stiffness/flexibility. Occurrence: **2019-Jan Q17(c)** — MS: *"The concrete is less flexible Or the concrete is stiffer (Do not accept a greater Young modulus)"*. Fix: extend the existing Not-accepted bullet: when a question asks *why* a treatment helps, answer in stiffness/flexibility/breaking-stress language, not "greater Young modulus". **Outside window** (2019-Jan).
