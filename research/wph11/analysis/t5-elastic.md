## 2. The sentences the mark schemes keep printing (statement by statement)

- 27: *Same force acts through both springs* · *Both springs have the same
  extension* · *In this combination the (total) extension will be doubled* ·
  *Reference to F = k∆x e.g. k is proportional to 1/∆x so k is halved*
  (Oct20 Q16(c)). *Stiffness = change in force ÷ change in length* (Jun22 Q10).
  *Springs in parallel are stiffer than by themselves* (Jun26-01A Q9).
- 28: *the mass is not needed* · *radius cannot be directly measured* (Jan23 Q9).
  *Diameter is required to calculate stress* (Jan23 Q9 ER). *doubling the
  diameter gives four times the cross section, requiring four times the
  tension for the same stress* (Jan22 Q8). *Lesser diameter gives greater
  stress and thus greater strain, greater length gives greater extension*
  (Jun23 Q3). *The most common error was for candidates to use the new
  length of the sample instead of using the change in length* (Jan25 Q17 ER).
- 29: *Maximum value of weight/force for which weight/force is proportional
  to extension* (Oct21 Q14(a)). *It's the force / stress beyond which the
  cable does not return to its original length when the force / stress is
  removed* (Jun22 Q12(a)). *Stress (or strain) value at/beyond which a
  material/object undergoes a sudden or large plastic deformation*
  (Jun23 Q20(a)). *The spring constant/stiffness (not Young modulus) is
  smaller or has changed* (Oct20 Q16(a)(iii)). *Same shape graph but starting
  from an extension > 0* (Oct20 Q16(b)). *The spring does not return to its
  original length* (Jan25 Q4). *F = k(L − L₀) … the gradient is equal to k*
  (Jan24 Q7 ER).
- 30: *Breaking stress is the stress at the end of each line. Yield stress is
  at the top of the initial straight section of each line* (Jan24 Q9 ER).
  *Stress = 110 (MPa) (allow answers in the range 108 to 112 (MPa))*
  (Jan25 Q17). *E = 1.2 ± 0.05 × 10¹¹ Pa* (Jan21 Q16). *388 MPa ≈ 400 MPa,
  so the running blade is made from carbon fibre* (Oct25 Q16).
  *Most candidates did not realise that breaking stress was unchanged*
  (Jun23 Q20(c) ER).
- 31: the Jun24 Q16 IC set (kept); *For long(er) wire, the extension will be
  large(r)* · *extension is proportional to the original length* · *extension
  is inversely proportional to cross-sectional area* · *A small(er) load can
  be used with a long/thin wire* (Jun19 Q16(c)).
- 32: *Elastic strain energy is equal to the area under graph* (Oct25 Q15(b)).
  *Energy stored = area under graph × volume (of rope)* (Jan25 Q17).
  *Those using an equation based approach often incorrectly substituted a
  change in force and a change in compression into the equation* (Jun25 Q15 ER).
  *There is some energy stored in the spring when the bread is in the toaster
  Or Some work is done by gravity* (Jan26 Q19(a)).
  *P is spurious and R is not included* (Oct22 Q6).
  *(So) elastic strain energy = F²/2k* (Oct25 Q15(b)).

## 3. Examiner-report lines that carry the teaching (new to this note)

- Oct21 Q14(a): *"There was no sense of this being a limit not a single point
  where the extension is proportional to the force."* (already in the note)
- Jan25 Q17(a): *"The most common error was for candidates to use the new
  length of the sample instead of using the change in length."*
- Jan25 Q17(b)(ii): *"This question proved challenging for many candidates,
  with slightly less than 40% scoring any marks."*
- Jun25 Q15(a): *"Those using an equation based approach often incorrectly
  substituted a change in force and a change in compression into the
  equation, where they should have substituted into the equation twice and
  determined the difference in values of energy calculated."*
- Jun25 Q15(b): *"many appeared to be defining the wrong term, with answers
  indicating that students were describing the elastic limit, or plastic
  deformation or another term from this area of the specification."*
- Jun25 Q17(a): *"they did not give a numerical comparison of their calculated
  value with the value in the question, so did not score the final mark.
  A statement that 2727 is closest to 2700 would have been enough … In
  'deduce' type questions, a numerical comparison is often needed to score
  the final mark."*
- Jun24 Q16(a): *"descriptions of how to measure the quantities needed had a
  tendency to be vague. For example, it was reasonably common for candidates
  to 'measure the extension' without making it clear what they were actually
  measuring."* (already in the note)
- Jun23 Q20(c): *"Most candidates did not realise that breaking stress was
  unchanged, and often stated that greater stress was required to break the
  new cable despite this being clearly contradicted by the question."*
- Oct24 Q18(c)(ii): *"Some candidates who answered in terms of the area under
  the graph … incorrectly stated that the area under the graph is equal to
  the elastic strain energy rather than being proportional to the elastic
  strain energy."* — note the MS itself prints *proportional to area under
  graph*; both routes credited.
- Oct24 Q17(b): *"A very common response was from candidates who used F = kΔx
  with the force being the weight of the student. This approach would
  effectively lead to candidates finding the equilibrium position."*
