# Scenario register guardrails — revised Topic 1 (WPH11)

Owner-dictated, 2026-11-25. Every scenario section in
`notes-ph01r-a.ts` / `notes-ph01r-b.ts` must be built exactly this
way. The same rules apply to any future scenario added anywhere in
the revised notes.

## The golden template (owner's words, verbatim)

Title: `TWO OBJECTS THROWN IN A VACUUM IN DIFFERENT DIRECTIONS`

```
In a vacuum , two objects are thrown in different directions with same initial speed from the same height
Weight is the only force that will act on an object in a vacuum
So since it's in a vacuum , both objects will have same acceleration downwards due to g
There are no other forces acting on the objects
As both objects are dropped from the same height , they will both have same Kinetic energy as they reach the floor
The only difference is that the two objects will have different velocity
Velocity is a vector quantity and magnitude and direction
So two objects thrown in different DIRECTIONS will obviously have different VELOCITY
Acceleration is a vector too but both accelerations have the same magnitude and the same downward direction
Therefore, two objects thrown from same height with same initial speed in a vacuum will have same acceleration, same KE as they fall but different velocity
```

## The guardrails

1. **Name the section after the physical situation** — "A ball
   falling through the air", "A car braking to a stop". Never name
   it after the exam topic or with meta words ("When air
   resistance matters" is banned).
2. **Follow the template spine.** The situation → the force or law
   that runs it → one consequence per line, each growing out of the
   previous line → the contrast ("The only difference is..." /
   "But...") → the keyword reason (vector, scalar, resultant
   force...) → "Therefore," + the full conclusion in one sentence.
3. **One line = one spoken sentence**, 8–16 words, the way you
   would say it to a student across a table. No fragments ("The
   curve flattens." says nothing usable). No double-barrelled
   lines. The final "Therefore," line may be longer — it recaps.
4. **Plain words, plain verbs:** gives, makes, means, increases,
   decreases, reduces, same, different, obviously, it's, that's.
   **Banned vocabulary:** grows, shrinks (owner ruling 2026-11-25:
   always say increases, decreases or reduces), consequently,
   furthermore,
   moreover, additionally, thus, hence, "due to the fact that",
   "in order to", "is determined by", "represents", "corresponds
   to", "fixes" (for "sets"), "utilize", "possess", "essentially",
   "basically", "note that", "it is important to note", "This
   implies", "In summary", "the exam's version of this".
5. **Connectives only from the owner's set:** So, Since, As, But,
   Then, That's why, The only difference is that, Therefore.
6. **Keep the physics keywords exact:** weight, resultant force,
   acceleration, g, kinetic energy (KE), velocity, speed, vector,
   scalar, magnitude, direction, terminal velocity, displacement,
   component, upthrust, drag, tension.
7. **No exam meta inside the teaching lines** — no talk about
   marks, mark schemes, sessions or "the exam's version of this".
   Mark-scheme wording lives only in the Mark scheme wording /
   Examiner's report / Not accepted note blocks, quoted verbatim,
   with no session tags in the teaching body.
8. **No new facts that are not in the syllabus point.** If a
   clarifying line is needed, it clarifies what is already there —
   it never opens a new topic.
9. **Read-aloud test before saving:** if a 15-year-old would stop
   and re-read a line, split it or say it plainer. If two
   neighbouring lines each carry half of one thought, join or
   rebuild them on the spine.

## What this replaces (the failure modes seen on 2026-11-25)

- **Chopping, not simplifying** — tiny fragments with the causal
  chain deleted. The template's lines are short *and* connected;
  one without the other is not the register.
- **Exam-meta voice** — "The table tennis ball is the exam's
  version of this", "The mark scheme grants no tolerance" inside
  teaching lines.
- **Generic topic headings** — "When air resistance matters",
  "Speed and velocity on a curve" instead of the situation.
- **No closer** — scenario trails off instead of ending with the
  "Therefore," sentence a student could lift into an exam.
- **Quiet physics errors** — e.g. a liquid fall that opens with
  "the only force is the weight" while later using upthrust; the
  opening must list all three forces (weight, drag, upthrust).

## Second batch of owner rulings (2026-11-25, same day)

- **Never "grows" / "shrinks"** anywhere in the notes. Always
  increases, decreases or reduces.
- **Terminal velocity phrasing:** write "The object reaches its
  terminal velocity" — never "the velocity now stays constant at
  terminal velocity".
- **A liquid fall must show the resultant-force chain**, not just
  assert it: the resultant force is W − U − D; as the drag
  increases, the resultant force decreases; since F = ma, as the
  resultant force decreases, the acceleration decreases. A student
  could not otherwise know where the resultant went.
- **Change of velocity is re-emphasised with the equation:**
  Δv = v_final − v_initial (final minus initial, signs carried),
  then a = Δv/Δt — since the change in velocity is negative, the
  acceleration is negative as well, because acceleration is the
  rate of change of velocity.
- **Bounce areas phrasing:** "If the areas are equal, the
  displacement is 0 — that is, the object has returned to its
  starting height. If the areas are unequal, the ball has bounced
  higher or lower than its starting height." Displacement is the
  straight line from start to end — the difference between the
  starting position and the final position, so the difference is 0.
- **Section titles name the situation, never a meta-test.**
  "The unit test" is banned as generic AI style — the unit section
  is titled "Which quantity has this unit?".
